A few perfect cabinets, if I may: the [winged altarpieces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veit_Stoss_altarpiece_in_Krak%C3%B3w#/media/File:Church_of_Our_Lady_Assumed_into_Heaven,_St._Mary's_(Main)_Altar_(1489_by_Veit_Stoss),_5_Mariacki_square,_Old_Town,_Krak%C3%B3w,_Poland.jpg) of the northern renaissance, particularly those by Veit Stoss and Nikolaus Hagenauer - and alongside them, the [dream structures](https://www.thisiscolossal.com/2015/09/paper-architecture-brodsky-utkin/) that Brodsky and Utkin etched into grids and mailed to Tokyo. Then this stunning [case](https://collections.soane.org/object-m470-a) (with its exposed brass ribs, dappled glass, casters as tall and elegant as heels) that holds the sarcophagus of Sety I in the Sepulchral Chamber of Sir John Soane's Holborn townhouse. The two-story chasm in a central Tokyo tower that preserves an [Imperial natural history collection](https://www.intermediatheque.jp/en); both the new [V&amp;A East Storehouse shelves](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/about-va-east-storehouse) (including the long approach through the badlands of post-2012 Stratford, that fulcrum of contemporary British history) and the vast steel armature of the Scottish National Museum's [entry hall](https://trove.henryweikel.net/aleph/2025-11-26_IMG_3689.jpeg) that preceded them, from which emerge hunks of coral, motorcycles, bone china, and propellers. That [backlit chamber](https://trove.henryweikel.net/aleph/kate2-e1605180327439-768x1024.jpg) of Victorian microscope slides in UCL's Grant Museum of Zoology. Joseph Cornell's [shadow boxes](https://www.artic.edu/artists/34033/joseph-cornell) more than Duchamp's [valise](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/371723), if only because Cornell's retain the cramped intensity of the unheated house he shared with his mother and brother in Queens. Reinhard Behrens' [feverish documentation](https://naboland.co.uk/253-2/) of his voyages into Naboland, David Jien's [pencil drawings](https://trove.henryweikel.net/aleph/jien.png), William Kentridge's [projection theaters](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/111012), June Leaf's [unnameable devices](https://trove.henryweikel.net/aleph/2025-07-05_IMG_1437.jpeg).

The chests of all alchemists and apothecaries. Korean [chaekgeori](https://www.clevelandart.org/articles/chaekgeori) obviously, but also the arrangement of shadow puppets in a casement window I passed once at dusk in Seattle - and the [neat gallery](https://trove.henryweikel.net/aleph/IMG_0570.JPG) of pollarded plane trees beneath Berkeley's Campanile (through the branches of which we circulated like swollen gibbons at 17). Certain books - surely Moby Dick and The Magic Mountain, the well wrought urns of digression - and some websites and many animations, like those by Yuri Norstein, the Brothers Quay, Phil Tippet, and Takehide Hori to start. The [whole peninsula of terraformed marshland](https://www.noguchi.org/artworks/public-works/view/moerenuma-park/) outside Sapporo that Noguchi scattered with unreal copses and geometric playgrounds. The purple velvet box that my mother keeps behind a sequined shroud, with one of its four padded compartments holding the whiskers of a lion gathered by my father.

Gene Wolfe writes, in [one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorare_(novella)) of his hundred perfect stories, of mortuary chambers launched into space, encased in asteroids or skinned with sheet metal. Decades later, a producer of pulp TV films himself spelunking into these memorials - floating cabinets arranged by and for the dead, waiting patiently to perform their compositions for the occasional visitor.

If there's a heuristic here, it's in systems neurotically arranged then lightly broken, near-misses at perfect taxonomies, the blinding compulsion (despite others' bafflement) to isolate and frame a specific fantasy like a section taken through the world.

And maybe it's miserly of me, but I think there's a size limit. As soon as a cabinet is immediately useful to too many people, it strikes me as having become something else, an archive or social network maybe. My father, a carpenter and the most prolific cabinetmaker of this sort I know, finds the more professionalized version finicky, almost unbearable. Because unlike kitchen cabinets, nothing listed here is broadly useful to anyone who might turn up - at least not in a way that can be anticipated. It's always a bit disorienting, the machinery of nostalgia and ritualized immersion. A good cabinetmaker of this school would need to dabble in essentially every human skill. It would take a full life I think to make one or two good ones; and they would in turn become the vessels of that life, compressed and rearranged, hurtling forward for centuries to come.

I'm doing my best. I have the skeletons of seven or eight on and around my desk right now, some inherited, others assembled from scraps or recovered from the side of the road. I like to think of them as broken optical instruments, awaiting only-partial repair. I've made sketches and fumbling first passes - the Aleph, a small tool for future cabinetmaking that holds every piece of digital imagery to my name, and [this arrangement](https://www.henryweikel.net/) of some beloved objects from my desk, made to serve as a mute little website.

I’d like to break the next one out of the screen for good: encase it in a block of acrylic, mount it with mylar sails, and fire it into the stars. More modestly, I might first put it on some casters and call it The Night Country.
